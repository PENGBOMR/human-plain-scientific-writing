## 1. Abstract — Direct Context -> Contradiction -> Method -> Numbers -> Plain Implication

### Exemplar 1A (Full 6-Slot Synthesis Abstract — 141 words, 6 sentences, median 23w)
> Mountain snowpacks supply summer streamflow to millions of downstream residents and strongly regulate river nitrate export. However, field studies have reported both rising and falling stream nitrate concentrations during warmer winters, creating debate over how snowpack loss alters watershed nitrogen retention. We built a catchment synthesis model using long-term water chemistry records from 142 mountain watersheds and paired soil frost measurements across temperate and boreal zones. Our model predicted observed winter nitrate export with an accuracy of 83% across independent validation sites. Earlier snowmelt reduced winter soil insulation and increased annual nitrate export in 64% of sampled watersheds, raising mean river nitrate loads by 18% (Table 1). If winter warming continues at current rates, downstream water utilities will likely face higher spring nitrate peaks and greater treatment costs.

**Why it works**: Zero hype adjectives, explicit subjects (`Mountain snowpacks`, `field studies`, `We`, `Our model`, `Earlier snowmelt`), plain workhorse verbs (`supply`, `reported`, `built`, `predicted`, `reduced`, `increased`), and a grounded conditional `If ... will likely ...` ending.

## 2. Introduction — Plain Concept Explanation & Concrete Gap

### Exemplar 2A (Explaining a Mechanism & Why Existing Indicators Fail)
> In cold alpine catchments, thick winter snowpacks insulate the soil and keep microbial activity low during freezing months. However, when winter temperatures rise and snowpacks thin, exposed surface soils undergo repeated freeze-thaw cycles that break down soil aggregates and kill fine roots. These physical and biological losses release dissolved nitrate into snowmelt water (Fig. 1). Because standard stream gauges rarely measure winter soil temperature, watershed models often miss this freeze-thaw nitrate pulse. Long-term paired snow and stream records compiled since the 1990s now allow us to test how snowpack thickness controls winter nitrate export across mountain climates.

**Why it works**: Everyday words (`insulate`, `thin`, `break down`, `kill`, `release`, `miss`), one cause per sentence, and smooth `These physical and biological losses...` chaining without adverb spam.

### Exemplar 2B (Parallel Contrast Hypothesis Build)
> Under light grazing, livestock remove senescent grass shoots and allow more sunlight to reach young leaves. Root growth often increases under this moderate defoliation, adding more organic matter to topsoil layers. Under heavy grazing, however, repeated trampling compacts the surface soil and reduces water infiltration. Plant cover drops sharply, and bare soil loses carbon through wind erosion and faster microbial breakdown. As a result, moderate and heavy grazing should shift grassland soil carbon in opposite directions, with a threshold separating carbon gain from carbon loss.

**Why it works**: Clean parallel framing (`Under light grazing, ...` / `Under heavy grazing, ...`), short flat sentences, explicit subjects and verbs throughout.

## 5. Discussion — Clear Deductive Logic Without Pseudo-Profound Phrasing

### Exemplar 5A (Deductive Refutation of an Alternative Hypothesis)
> Soil mineral composition may help explain why subalpine catchments retain more nitrate than steep alpine catchments. Moving from alpine to subalpine basins, stream nitrate export stayed low across a wider range of snow depths (Fig. 4a, b). Compared with low-deposition basins, high-deposition basins exported nitrate at shallower freezing depths (Supplementary Fig. S4). Background atmospheric deposition therefore may not be the main driver of elevation differences; otherwise, subalpine sites receiving heavier deposition would have shown earlier nitrate losses. Instead, thicker mineral soils and higher clay content in subalpine valleys likely allow stronger microbial retention during thaw.

**Why it works**: Crystal-clear deductive chain (`therefore may not be the main driver ...; otherwise, ... would have shown ... . Instead, ... likely allow ...`).
